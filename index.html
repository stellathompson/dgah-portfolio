Welcome!

Who am I?

Hello! I'm Stella Thompson (she/her). Originally from Columbus, Ohio, I am graduating from Carleton College this spring with a major in Computer Science and minors in Music and Digital Arts and Humanities. At school, I have enjoyed working as a Prefect for the Computer Science department and a Student Manager in the dining halls. For fun, I play on the Club Tennis team, where I'm the Treasurer, practice the Pipe Organ, and host a weekly student radio show.

What is Digital Arts and Humanities?

Digital Arts and Humanities is an interdisciplinary field at the intersection between digital methods and the Humanities. In addition to exploring how computational tools can enhance traditional humanities work, digital humanities also seeks to understand the impacts of using such tools. At Carleton, this includes a range of courses in departments such as Computer Science, History, English, Archaeology, and Cinema and Media Studies. You can read more about the Digital Arts and Humanities program at Carleton here.

How do we fit together?

I chose to come to Carleton, a Liberal Arts College, so that I could explore a wide range of classes and interests. Even though I settled on a Computer Science major, I really enjoy humanistic inquiry and wanted to incorporate humanities classes into my studies. The digital arts and humanities program is perfect for me because it allows me to apply my technical skills to humanities projects. Ideally, I would like to pursue a career in digital archiving or museum work so that I could continue merging these interests.

Junk Mail Mobile App

Description

For this project, I worked with five other students over the course of 6 months to research, design, and develop a mobile app in React Native. The project was in collaboration with the Office of Health Promotion and we were creating an app for their campus sexual health supply service called Junk Mail. Check out the project here!

Takeaways

This was by far the most rigorous project I've worked on and I really enjoyed working on the front-end team to create an accessible, usable, and attractive user interface. It was also a valuable experience working closely with a community partner to ensure that their needs for the project were met. I had fun combining creative design with functional technology.

Wordle

Description

For my final project in Intro to CS, I worked with a partner to create a replica of the game Wordle. Using Python, we divided the work into graphic design and backend logic. We even came up with a simple AI mode where the computer could made an educated guess based on the outcome of previous guesses. You can see an image of our final product to the left.

Takeaways

This was my first big coding project. I really enjoyed collaborating with my partner and creating something that was usable (and fun!). The class sparked my interest in using technology to create.

Video Game Music Composition

Description

For my Video Game Music seminar, we created digital compositions using Petaporon for a video game produced by Jane Parson. I went with a retro 8-bit sound and had 3 tracks: menu, gameplay, and death cue.

Takeaways

I enjoyed creating music with strict limitations, and I certainly respect video game music composers more after this assignment. It is really difficult to compose something that loops perfectly, and that's an important part of game music, since you don't know what the user will do and how long it will take. The tool we used, Petaporon, was a fun, easy way to create digital compositions.

Loads Laundry App

Description

For my Human-Computer Interaction course, we identified a campus problem and designed an app to solve it. This included rigorous user-testing and research. Our project was "Loads," an app to streamline communal laundry at Carleton. The video shows my final prototype, and the image is an earlier iteration to show how research impacted my design. I've always been interested in user-experience and design, and this was my first formal foray into the field. The project definitely cemented my interest in working on human-centered technology projects.

Personal Website

Description

Part of my Human-Computer Interaction course involved implementing a personal website. This served as an exercise in accessible, usable design, as well as a practical tool for getting internships and jobs. We used GitHub Pages to host our sites and I used a Bootstrap template to get me started. The website you are currently on builds off of my personal website, which is still in use.

Takeaways

I had fun infusing my personality into the website while also considering tenets of usability. Being attentive to alt text, skip to main content, and other accessibility features made me realize how much work it is to maintain a truly accessible site. This was also good practice working in HTML and CSS, skills that are important for web development.

Exploration of Video Game Music

Description

For my Video Game Music course, I produced a 20-page research paper about sound in the video game Journey. My focus was the "chirp" sound that players can trigger their avatar to make. I argued that the chirp served as a liminal sound that defied strict categorization as diegetic or non-diegetic.

Takeaways

Since I'm not a gamer, I learned a lot about the interplay between the technology and artistry behind video games in this course. The final paper allowed me to analyze the digital art form of gaming, and I was especially excited to work with digital sound, since I am a Music minor.

Mapping Faribault

Description

In my Deep Mapping course, we worked with the Rice County Historical Society to record the history of the Alexander Faribault House in Faribault, Minnesota. My partner and I worked on creating an index map of Faribault using Sanborn Fire Insurance maps. After georeferencing the maps in ArcGIS Pro, we created a digital map in ArcGIS Online. Read about us in the news or check out our final project!

Takeaways

I loved learning to use ArcGIS Pro for this project and it sparked my interest in using GIS in the future. It was also really fulfilling to work with a community partner and work on something that I knew would be useful to RCHS staff.

Deep Map of Northfield

Description

Using Google MyMaps, I created a deep map of two blocks in Northfield. This deep map captures the experience of being in place rather than just physical attributes. You can read my deep map blog post here!

Takeaways

It was fun to use MyMaps since it is such a simple mapping tool. I also felt that, through the project, I got a better sense of what a deep map is. It was nice to take my time walking around Northfield and take in my surroundings.

The Tate Data Visualization

Description

For my midterm project in Hacking the Humanities, I chose to create data visualizations using data from The Tate Modern museum. I used OpenRefine and Google Sheets to organize the data and then used Flourish to create the visualizations. I aimed to tell a story through my visualizations about inequities in The Tate's collection. Check out the final product here!

Takeaways

Before Hacking the Humanities, I had never done data visualization, so I learned a lot of new tools for this project. I enjoyed exploring the data set to extract meaning from it and had fun customizing the appearance. The project gave me more confidence in working with data.

The Tome Library

Description

For my final project in Hacking the Humanities, I worked with other students to record the history of the Carleton English Department's Reading Marathons. We digitized books from Carleton's archives, collected oral histories and artifacts, and then catalogued them all into an Omeka website. Check out our final product here!

Takeaways

Using Omeka was frustrating because the user-interface was much harder to customize than other software I had used. The process of researching and finding artifacts was fun, however, and compiling it all into one central site is a useful skill for a potential career in archives.

Election Data Website

Description

Over the course of a term, I worked with 4 other students to implement a data-driven website that displayed election demographic information to users. After a user selected a state and county, we visualized how different demographics voted using a pie-chart. We worked in Python, HTML, CSS, and PostgreSQL. Check out our repo here!

Takeaways

This was only my third CS class at Carleton, and I was excited to try out software design, a potential career for any CS major. While I don't anticipate going into software development, I learned a lot of valuable skills in project management, data, and the relationship between front-end and back-end. The project was so fun that I decided to declare CS as my major!

Digital Composition

Description

For my Music minor, I took Composition Studio, where we were encouraged to explore both acoustic and digital composition. I went digital for the first assignment, using LogicPro to produce a piece that "leveled up," a term my professor invented for songs that morph into something unexpected.

Takeaways

There are a lot of capabilities in LogicPro that I didn't take advantage of because it is such a robust piece of software but I found it relatively easy to get started. Having a tool that allows you to produce music from a laptop is awesome. I definitely prefer acoustic music but enjoyed exploring digital forms of music-making for this assignment.

Archaeology Grant Proposal

Description

For our Digital Archaeology final project, my group created a fake grant proposal to do an archaeological investigation of My Little Pony land. We figured out how to apply the various technologies we learned about to draw conclusions about how animals lived in the time before segregation between ponies and unicorns.

Takeaways

This project was a great way to take our learning out of the classroom (metaphorically). After hearing about so many methods and tools, we were able to "apply" then to a "real-life" scenario to understand how they all fit together. This course sparked my interest in studying historical artifacts and understanding the past using digital tools.
